# Steam-native opportunities, festivals, press and creators as wishlist drivers (2025-2026, online co-op focus)

Context: Hell Bros (small Peruvian indie) / "Look Out: Indie Game", asymmetrical online co-op 3D platformer for exactly 3 players, unannounced Steam page, demo and trailer in progress. Research date: 1 Nov 2026.

Evidence labels used below: CONFIRMED = stated on a primary source I fetched (Steamworks, organizer). SURVEY = self-reported developer data. ESTIMATE = third-party estimate. SNIPPET = seen only in a search summary, page not read in full. Anything older than 2025 carries its year.

## Steam Next Fest: wishlist gains, predictors and the 2027 calendar

### Takeaway
A typical surveyed game earns about 800-1,000 wishlists from Next Fest, and pre-fest wishlists are the strongest predictor: games entering with fewer than 1,000 wishlists had a median of 322. Two 2027 editions are confirmed (22 Feb-1 Mar, registration closes 10 Jan; 14-21 Jun, registration closes 25 Apr); October 2027 is planned but undated, and a game gets only one Next Fest.

**What predicts results**
- Total wishlists correlated with fest wishlists more strongly than velocity (wishlists in the 14 days before the fest); no coefficients on that page — [How To Market A Game, Apr 2026](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/). A secondary source gives Spearman r = 0.825 between pre-fest wishlists and fest wishlists — [Ziva (SNIPPET)](https://ziva.sh/blogs/steam-next-fest-2026).
- Streamer coverage is cited as a driver for middle-tier games — [How To Market A Game, Apr 2026](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/).
- Visibility mechanics: registration categories and Steam Tags determine sorting; the first days are randomized apart from named lists and personalized segments, then logged-in users see carousels personalized by what similar users played; the Wrap-Up features the most-played demos — [Steamworks Next Fest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest).
- Co-op is currently overrepresented at the top: Mistfall Hunter, The Mound, Bombanana!, Over The Hill and Grain Rot were all named among June 2026 leaders, and top-15 wishlist games shared "co-op" and "online co-op" tags. GameDiscoverCo stops short of naming one predictor — [GameDiscoverCo, June 2026](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/who-won-june-2026s-steam-next-fest).
- Co-op friendslop games dominated Feb 2026 charts and had the lowest plays-to-wishlists ratio, per the author's earlier post — [How To Market A Game, Apr 2026](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/).
- Zukowski recommends entering the last Next Fest before launch — [How To Market A Game, Apr 2026](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/).

### Inferences
- Feb 2027 looks too tight: registration is 70 days away, the page is still unannounced, and the demo must be through review by 18 Jan to catch Press Preview. Since a game gets one Next Fest, spending it with few wishlists would likely land in the 322-median band.
- June 2027 or October 2027 is the more defensible slot. Choose based on release date, because the advice is to use the last fest before launch.
- Registering for February carries low risk if the team wants to keep the option open, because Valve allows an opt-out for an unreleased game. Check the form's terms first.
- Working target (my reading of the table): pass 1,000 pre-fest wishlists at minimum; 10,000+ moves the median expectation to about 5,000.
- Flag for a small studio: the store page must be public well before the fest, so "unannounced" has to end before the 10 May review step at the latest for June.

### Gaps
- No October 2026 percentile data or GameDiscoverCo October analysis was found; the fest ended only days ago.
- No wishlist percentiles split by co-op vs. non-co-op.
- HTMAG's sample size for the percentile table is not stated. A "2,000 wishlists is where Next Fest starts working" threshold appeared in a search summary attributed to Zukowski, but I did not verify it on a page.
- October 2027 dates are unpublished.

## Public demo before Next Fest, demo reviews and player counts

### Takeaway
A public demo before the fest helps directionally: surveyed games with early demos earned about 2.5x more wishlists, though the correlation is weak (r = -0.205). Demo reviews and their visibility effect are poorly documented; Valve says demos surface like free games, and a separate demo page shows no meaningful wishlist advantage in a small 2024 survey.

### Cited Findings
- Games whose demos launched well before the promotion window earned about 2.5x more wishlists on average, but correlation between timing and wishlists was weak (r = -0.205) with heavy overlap, and some demos launched less than a month before still did well — [How To Market A Game benchmarks](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/).
- Of 10 "Super Earners" (15,000+ fest wishlists) in the Feb 2026 survey: 5 had demos out months before, 4 launched about a month before, and 1 surprise-dropped days before — [How To Market A Game, Apr 2026](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/).
- Word Play's demo launched about 2 weeks before Next Fest and reportedly helped gain about 25,000 wishlists before the fest began, and the early release let the developer fix issues — SNIPPET, year not verified, [GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/how-to-make-a-great-steam-next-fest).
- Valve permits it: a demo can go live any time after approval, as long as it is live before the fest. Valve recommends at least 30 minutes before start. Releasing before Press Preview makes it public to everyone — [Steamworks Next Fest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest).
- Demo updates after approval are not reviewed, and build review "is not a full QA process" — [Steamworks Next Fest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest).
- Avoid running a Playtest during Next Fest, since it splits players between two builds. Consider keeping the demo up after the fest — [Steamworks Next Fest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest).
- One-shot wishlist notification: after a demo first becomes playable, you can email and notify wishlisters once, within 14 days of first release; disabling and re-enabling does not reset it. There is a two-week cooldown per App ID — [Steamworks Demos](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/application/demos).
- Demo reviews exist only if the optional separate demo store page is enabled. Demos get no community hub either way — [Steamworks Demos](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/application/demos).
- "Demos can appear anywhere in Steam that a free game could appear", including New & Trending, hubs, tag and genre pages — [Steamworks Demos](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/application/demos).
- 2024 coverage reported that a separate page gives no visibility advantage, and that a demo with many negative reviews could be filtered from some views — SNIPPET, 2024, [GameWorldObserver](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/07/26/steam-game-demos-update-reviews-store-pages-charts).
- A small 2024 comparison found demo on main page 967 median wishlists vs. separate demo page 930 — SNIPPET, 2024, [How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/11/04/how-was-the-october-2024-steam-next-fest/).
- Conversion is a noisy metric: median share of demo players who wishlisted was 16.33% in Feb 2026 (SNIPPET, [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-next-fest-2026-dates)) and in June 2026 was 15.9% / 19.3% / 23.2% at 30th/50th/70th percentile, with the trendline rising only slightly against total wishlists — [How To Market A Game, Jun 2026](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/06/30/nobody-plays-demos-and-that-is-ok/).
- Roadside Research's demo reached 903,000 unique players with a median session of about 1h12m, and the game had 250,000+ wishlists by Sept 2025 — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-two-friendslop-hits-got-the-audience).

### Inferences
- Plan the sequence: publish the page, build a wishlist base, release the demo, use the one-shot notification, then enter the fest. The notification is worth more the larger the list is when the demo ships.
- A trio-only demo has a specific failure mode: a lone player cannot experience the game. Public lobbies, a looking-for-group path, and tested online stability matter more here than in solo demos, and an early public demo is the only safe way to test that under real load.
- The bad-review filter claim is reason enough to hold off on a separate demo page until the online experience is stable, given the near-zero wishlist difference.

### Gaps
- Valve does not state whether most-played rank boosts visibility during the fest, or whether demo reviews affect discovery. A January 2026 developer thread asking whether review-less demos are penalized was unresolved ([Reddit snapshot, SNIPPET](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1qcnl03/snapshots/2026-01-15T01%3A10%3A47.75936Z)).
- No quantified link between demo review score and wishlists was found.
- No data on whether group-only demos underperform, or on looking-for-group tools in demos.

## Press: does it still move wishlists?

### Takeaway
Yes, in spikes and when tied to a demo or Next Fest, but no rigorous evidence by outlet size exists. The available numbers are vendor case studies and anecdotes, and they consistently place press behind creators for direct wishlists.

### Cited Findings
- **Mainstream non-gaming news (2023):** Zukowski concludes being covered by regular news does not help wishlists; blog-level, not controlled — [How To Market A Game, 2023](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/04/17/what-happens-when-the-mainstream-media-covers-indie-games/).
- **Large gaming outlets:** a PR firm reports Bore Blasters rising from 3,000 to 13,000+ wishlists during Next Fest with coverage from IGN, GamesRadar+, PC Gamer, PCGamesN, RPS and Steam Deck HQ, and 18K+ before launch. This is vendor-reported, year unstated, and bundled with an early demo and Next Fest, so press cannot be isolated — [UberStrategist](https://uberstrategist.com/case-studies/8bitskull-bore-blasters-pr).
- **Single article estimate:** a PC Gamer article "might generate 500 wishlists", an unsourced vendor estimate; the same guide says creators often drive more wishlists than press — [presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/how-to-get-press-coverage-indie-game).
- **Reposted viral trailer:** IGN and GameSpot re-uploads of a viral trailer were tied to 85,000+ wishlists in two weeks; vendor-authored, and the trailer was already viral — [presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/how-to-get-press-coverage-indie-game).
- **Regional press:** one developer reported 2,000+ wishlists in a day from Japanese press, against little from Western coverage, a single anecdote — [Reddit snapshot, Dec 2025](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1pe60qh/snapshots/2025-12-05T10%3A30%3A22.350543Z).
- Next Fest offers a built-in press window: Press Preview starts 11 Feb (Feb edition) and 3 Jun (June edition) for demos that opt in and pass review on time — [Steamworks Feb 2027](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest/feb_2027); [Steamworks June 2027](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest/june_2027).
- PR timing example: Bore Blasters announced its release date on Day 2 of Next Fest to avoid the Day 1 news rush — [UberStrategist](https://uberstrategist.com/case-studies/8bitskull-bore-blasters-pr).

### Inferences
- Treat press as a multiplier for an existing beat (page reveal, demo, Next Fest), not a standalone driver. Low-cost email outreach is justified; paid PR is hard to justify on this evidence.
- A co-op press demo needs a plan for three reviewers playing together. Sending group keys or offering a scheduled session with the team is likely necessary, because a solo journalist cannot see the game. Reasoned, not sourced.
- LatAm-focused and Spanish/Portuguese outlets may be a cheap extra, by analogy with the Japanese anecdote. This is unverified.

### Gaps
- No controlled or before/after dataset by outlet size (AAA site, mid-size, niche, regional) was found for 2025-2026.
- No evidence specifically on co-op games and press.

## Creators: results, tools, co-op hits and the exactly-3-player question

### Takeaway
Recent co-op hits spread through watchability and repeat creator play rather than pre-launch wishlists: PEAK launched with only 29,384 wishlists and sold 4.5M in about three weeks. I found no direct evidence on games requiring exactly 3 players; the genre norm is flexible 1-4 or more, so a fixed trio is an untested friction for streamer adoption and should be mitigated in design.

### Cited Findings

**Co-op hits**
- **PEAK (Jun 2025):** launched 16 Jun 2025; teaser and page live about 4 days before launch; 29,384 wishlists at launch; $8, discounted to $5 for the first week; 4.5M copies by 8 Jul 2025; about 100,000 daily CCU; median playtime 3h53m. Shareability and watchers including influencers are described as a major driver, with emotes, ghost form, proximity voice and a daily-changing mountain. No specific streamers named — [GameDiscoverCo, Jul 2025](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-peak-sold-45m-copies-in-less). It rode existing platforms: Landfall's Content Warning peaked around 200k CCU — same source.
- Later PEAK totals conflict: 10M ([Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/amp/story/gaming/gaming-news/from-game-jam-to-10-million-units-indie-hit-peak-success)) and 23M from a podcast summary ([WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/zh/news/finance/item-52475)). These may be different dates; not reconciled.
- **Valve's advice:** Valve advised PEAK against the free 24-hour launch Content Warning used — [GameDiscoverCo, Jul 2025](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-peak-sold-45m-copies-in-less).
- **Super Battle Golf (Feb 2026, $8):** 500,000 units about 9 days after launch, nearing 800k. Top CCU came 9 days after launch. Some streamers returned 5-6 times in three weeks and creators ran their own tournaments — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-two-friendslop-hits-got-the-audience).
- **Roadside Research (Feb 2026, $13, 1-4 players):** 396,000 units, 250,000+ wishlists (Sept 2025), 903,000 demo players. Technically solo but "very tricky" without at least two players; 47% US, 5% Brazil. Hook: aliens undercover at a gas station, with fan-drawn disguises — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-two-friendslop-hits-got-the-audience).
- **Gamble With Your Friends (2026):** reported 500k in 3 days and 1M in a week — [inkl/Speechless](https://www.inkl.com/news/another-friendslop-hit-prints-money-on-steam-as-gamble-with-your-friends-sells-500k-copies-in-3-days-speechless); [GosuGamers](https://www.gosugamers.net/entertainment/news/78409-gamble-with-your-friends-sells-one-million-copies-in-a-week).
- **Over The Hill:** reportedly 650,000 wishlists in ten months, credited to consistency, social content and showcase momentum — SNIPPET, [Fix Gaming](https://www.fixgamingchannel.com/tag/off-road-exploration-games/). It was #5 by unique players in June 2026 Next Fest — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/who-won-june-2026s-steam-next-fest).
- **Lethal Company (2023):** four-player co-op that blew up in late 2023 after streamers showcased it — SNIPPET, friendslop explainers such as [Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/friendslop-games-why-these-multiplayer-hits-go-viral-and-fade-fast).
- **Genre framing:** low price, easy entry, fun depends on who you play with, and "most of these games support up to four players" so friend rotation matters — SNIPPET, [Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/friendslop-games-why-these-multiplayer-hits-go-viral-and-fade-fast); [Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/3d/video-game-design/what-is-friendslop-and-why-it-it-taking-over-gaming-in-2026).
- Friendslop has a short tail: PEAK's developers said it was not built to last forever — [Digital Citizen](https://www.digitalcitizen.life/peak-devs-not-forever-game/).

**Streamer results**
- Middle-class Next Fest results (2,000-3,000 wishlists) are associated with decent but not great streamer coverage, per Zukowski's gut feel — [How To Market A Game, Apr 2026](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/).

**Tools and cost**
- **Lurkit:** Indie, Studio and Publisher tiers, all "Contact for pricing". Indie includes insights, creator CRM, key campaigns and paid campaigns; paid Quests carry a 20% commission. ### Inferences
- **Exactly 3 players, likely effects (reasoned, not measured):**
  - Scheduling: a streamer needs two specific partners, not "whoever is around". Flexible 1-4 games let creators start solo or with one friend. This is the main adoption friction.
  - Upside: a trio is a natural content format, and an asymmetric role split gives each creator a distinct clip-worthy perspective, which plays to the shareability that PEAK and Super Battle Golf show. Creator groups of three exist, but so do groups of four, and a four-person group would leave someone out.
  - Viewer conversion: a watcher needs two friends to buy. This argues for very low pricing, the pattern here being $5-$13.
- **Mitigations to evaluate:** a bot or AI third role, a two-player mode, a spectator role for a fourth, a public matchmaking path, and team-led sessions where a Hell Bros developer fills a slot for creators. The last one is cheap and directly removes the friction during demo outreach.
- Prioritize small and mid creator groups who already play co-op together over solo mega-streamers, and pitch trios with three keys and a scheduled time. PEAK's result depended on existing audiences that Hell Bros does not have, so its low-wishlist launch should not be copied.
- Budget note: a low-cost path is direct email plus a free tier, since Lurkit's and Keymailer's paid figures are unverified. Confirm before paying.

### Gaps
- **No source addresses exactly-3-player games or streamer adoption by fixed group size.** This is the central unknown; a search for it returned only general friendslop content.
- No wishlist-per-view or co-op-specific creator conversion data was found.
- No usable analysis found for R.E.P.O., Schedule I, Content Warning launch mechanics, or Chained Together. Only weak material surfaced (e.g. [Massively OP's R.E.P.O. streams](https://massivelyop.com/tag/r-e-p-o/), which shows attention, not impact).
- I did not check official Keymailer/Partnier or Woovit pricing pages. No GDC talk was reviewed.
